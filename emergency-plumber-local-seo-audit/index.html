Diagnostic checklist for residential plumbing businesses

Audit the emergency-plumbing foundation before buying more SEO.

Emergency service pages often receive more content before anyone checks whether the profile, site structure, location signals, trust cues, and call path agree. A useful audit identifies those blockers first and turns them into a sequenced 30/60/90-day plan.

Start with evidence, not ranking promises. Review what a customer and nearby competitor can see today. Separate factual defects from longer-term experiments, and preserve anything already working.

The first seven diagnostic checks

1. Emergency availability

Verify that 24/7 or after-hours claims match the Google profile, website, phone handling, and real operating capability.

2. Profile-to-page match

Confirm that the linked landing page clearly supports the primary plumbing category, priority services, and actual service area.

3. Service architecture

Map drain, water-heater, leak, sewer, and emergency intent to useful pages instead of duplicating thin city-and-keyword templates.

4. Location model

Distinguish real staffed locations from service areas, remove ambiguous legacy addresses, and align public name, phone, and location facts.

5. Mobile call path

Test whether an urgent visitor can understand coverage, trust the business, and reach the correct phone or booking step without friction.

6. Review evidence

Check response quality, recency, service specificity, and whether critical reviews reveal operational gaps that the site should clarify.

7. Three-competitor comparison

Compare the strongest nearby plumbers on clarity, proof, service coverage, profile completeness, and conversion—not just keyword counts.

Turn findings into a 30/60/90-day plan

First 30 days: repair
  • Correct conflicting facts
  • Fix broken call and form paths
  • Clarify priority services
  • Resolve location ambiguity
Days 31–60: strengthen
  • Improve core service pages
  • Add credible job and team proof
  • Standardize review responses
  • Close competitor clarity gaps
Days 61–90: measure
  • Track qualified calls and forms
  • Test high-intent page changes
  • Expand only proven topics
  • Review profile and site drift

What the audit should not do

It should not promise rankings, invent locations, add keywords to the real-world business name, create fake reviews, or recommend dozens of near-duplicate pages without evidence. The deliverable should explain why each action is prioritized and which facts support it.
